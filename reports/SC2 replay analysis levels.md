# Six levels carry SC2 replays from schema to trends

A "level 0 to hero" tutorial over the s2protocol snapshot should climb six levels that mirror the order of R for Data Science and Python for Data Analysis: inspect one table (schema, nulls, duplicates, cardinalities), one game over time, many games aggregated over calendar time, trends on those aggregates, players compared, maps and races compared; every level shows one Polars lazy query next to its DuckDB SQL twin and asserts the two frames equal after sorting and casting. The cheapest and most robust way to get the SQL twin is to let DuckDB scan the Polars `LazyFrame` by variable name, which **works on the installed DuckDB 1.5.2 with projection and filter pushdown** (verified locally on 2026-11-04) and needs no community extension, whereas the `nanoarrow` extension that reads `.arrow` files directly is only built for DuckDB 1.4.4/1.4.5/1.5.5/1.5.6/2.0.0 ([nanoarrow community extension](https://duckdb.org/community_extensions/extensions/nanoarrow)). The metrics the StarCraft II community recognises and the snapshot can compute are economy metrics from `stats` (income, unspent, Spending Quotient `SQ = 35(0.00137 i − ln u) + 240`, workers at fixed minutes, spending split, army value, resources lost and killed and their ratio, and, because the real `stats` table carries `food_used`/`food_made`, supply-block time), build-order, expansion, upgrade and inject/chrono/MULE timings from `unit_born`, `upgrades` and the two command tables, kill heat maps from `unit_died`, and the `details`-based comparisons: win rate by matchup from each race's side, by map, by game-length bucket (0-4/4-10/10-15/15-20/20+ minutes), opening popularity per window, head-to-head records and per-player progression; APM, EPM, hotkey and camera metrics are not computable because the snapshot has no selection, control-group or camera events. The trends level should fit per-entity slopes with functime's `.ts.linear_trend()` and polars-ds `pds.lin_reg_report()` (the only Polars-native path to a p-value), weight weekly win rates by games played, show Wilson intervals, and refuse to call a trend on fewer than about ten points. Figures export headlessly with plotly 6.7.0 and kaleido 1.5.0 through a Chrome that `plotly_get_chrome` downloads once into `~/.local/share/choreographer/deps/`, launched with `--headless --no-sandbox --disable-gpu`; the `functime[plot]` extra must never be installed because it pins `kaleido==0.2.1`. The version landscape moved under the project in autumn 2026: DuckDB 2.0.0 LTS shipped on 2026-10-21 and Polars 2.0 (streaming by default, no group-by order guarantee) is at release-candidate stage, so the tutorial should pin `polars<2` and `duckdb<2` explicitly and name the version each feature needs.

## The level ladder follows R4DS and McKinney, with one engine pair per cell

The pedagogical sources agree on an order and the tutorial's six levels map onto it one to one. R for Data Science (2e, chapter 10) runs EDA as questions, then variation within one variable (typical values, unusual values), then missing values, then covariation (categorical x numerical, two categoricals, two numericals), then patterns and models, and frames EDA as "an iterative cycle" rather than "a formal process with a strict set of rules" ([R4DS, Exploratory data analysis](https://r4ds.hadley.nz/EDA.html)). Python for Data Analysis (3e) orders its chapters as loading, cleaning and preparation, wrangling (join, combine, reshape), plotting, aggregation and group operations, time series, modelling, and worked examples ([wesmckinney.com/book](https://wesmckinney.com/book/)). Practitioner guides front-load a schema and integrity pass, "expected columns, types, units, and allowed values; primary keys, duplicate rows, foreign-key joins, and orphan records", before any univariate or bivariate work ([livedocs, Python EDA guide](https://livedocs.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-python-exploratory-data-analysis-from-pandas-to-insights)). So **level 0 "inspect a table"** is the schema and integrity pass plus univariate variation: dtypes, `null_count`, exact and key duplicates on `ext_fs_id` (the replay key shared by every table), the cardinality of players, maps and `ext_protocol_version`, value ranges (dates inside the snapshot window, durations above zero), and orphan joins such as a replay present in `stats` but absent from `details`. **Level 1 "one game over time"** is the single-entity time series, exactly the scope of the archived IBM "StarCraft II Replay Analysis" code pattern, which plots mineral and vespene collection rate, active workers, supply utilisation and worker/supply ratio for two players side by side with Nelson-rule annotations (nine or more points on one side of the mean, six or more rising or falling) and explicitly "does not aggregate across games" ([IBM/starcraft2-replay-analysis](https://github.com/IBM/starcraft2-replay-analysis)). **Level 2 "many games over calendar time"** is McKinney's group operations plus Polars `group_by_dynamic` on `ext_datetime`; **level 3 "trends"** is R4DS's "patterns and models" applied to those aggregates; **levels 4 and 5 "compare players, compare maps and races"** are R4DS's covariation sections, boxplots or densities per player for a numeric metric, count tiles for player x map and the race x race matchup grid. No existing esports tutorial has this progression; the IBM notebook is the closest precedent and stops at level 1, so the ladder itself is new material.

Two structural rules make the ladder teachable. First, each level is "one question, one Polars query, one SQL query, one equality check, one figure", and the figure is saved as PNG at the end of the cell so the notebook also works under `nbconvert --execute`. Second, the trend level needs enough calendar span to be honest: the simulation literature finds that "the ability to detect trends in time series is hampered by the influence of autocorrelated residuals in short series lengths" of 10, 20 and 30 points ([NOAA IEA simulation study](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/62985)), and the Mann-Kendall implementations switch from exact tables to the normal approximation only at n >= 10 ([EarthSoft, Mann-Kendall](https://help.earthsoft.com/lib_mann-kendall.htm)), so level 2 must produce at least ten weekly points per entity before level 3 fits anything. Polars' `pl.int_range(pl.len()).over(entity)` gives the regression index, and `rolling_mean_by("week", window_size="8w")` gives a calendar-window smoother that tolerates missing weeks, both verified on polars 1.40.0.

The sanity checks that every source shares, and that level 0 should run on the seven tables, are: expected columns and dtypes, null counts per column, duplicates (exact rows and the replay key), value ranges, cardinality of categoricals, and join orphans between tables ([livedocs](https://livedocs.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-python-exploratory-data-analysis-from-pandas-to-insights); [Machine Learning Mastery, beginner EDA](https://machinelearningmastery.com/from-data-to-insights-a-beginners-journey-in-exploratory-data-analysis/)). R4DS adds the rule to replace unusual values with missing values rather than drop rows, and to repeat the analysis with and without outliers ([R4DS](https://r4ds.hadley.nz/EDA.html)); for the snapshot that means flagging the very short games (SQ's inventor says not to compute SQ "when average income is below 600 resources per minute", [Liquipedia, Spending quotient](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/Spending_quotient)) instead of filtering them silently.

## The snapshot computes SQ, supply blocks and trade efficiency, never APM

The community's replay metrics come from two layers: Blizzard's tracker events (PlayerStats every 10 game seconds, unit born/died/init/done, upgrades) and the raw game events (commands, selections, control groups, camera). The snapshot carries the first layer plus the command subset of the second, which fixes what is and is not computable. Patch 2.0.8 opened the tracker data for exactly these analyses, GGTracker's army-strength charts, "when a player began floating too many resources and how much was left unspent", and Spending Skill compared from Bronze to Grandmaster ([Blizzard, patch 2.0.8 replay enhancements](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/9669862/starcraft-ii-patch-2-0-8-replay-file-enhancements)). The PlayerStats fields decoded by sc2reader are `minerals/vespene_current`, the two collection rates, `workers_active_count`, the `used_in_progress`, `used_current`, `lost` and `killed` triples split army/economy/technology, `food_used`, `food_made`, `minerals/vespene_used_active_forces` and the friendly-fire fields ([sc2reader tracker.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggtracker/sc2reader/master/sc2reader/events/tracker.py)). The real `stats` table of the snapshot carries every one of them as `Int32`, including `food_used`, `food_made` and the active-forces and friendly-fire columns, plus `ext_replay_loop`, `ext_replay_seconds` and `ext_fs_id` (schema read locally from `~/SC2Replays/PRO/ipcs`, 2026-11-04); the research brief's assumption that supply was missing is wrong, and the supply values are already in supply units (maximum 215 over the snapshot), not sc2reader's raw `/4096` encoding.

The table below gives the formula, the source tables and the status of each metric. The time axis is settled: the engine runs 16 game loops per game second and Faster is a 1.4x factor, hence 22.4 loops per real second, and LotV displays real time everywhere ([Blizzard s2client-proto](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Blizzard/s2client-proto/master/docs/protocol.md); [Blizzard/s2protocol issue 70](https://github.com/Blizzard/s2protocol/issues/70#issuecomment-379000527); [Liquipedia, Game Speed](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/Game_Speed)). The snapshot's `ext_replay_seconds` equals `floor(ext_replay_loop / 22.4)` (maximum deviation 0.86 s on a local file, against 199 s for a 16-loop hypothesis) and PlayerStats rows step by exactly 160 loops, so the "every 10 seconds" of sc2reader ([sc2reader tracker events](https://sc2reader.readthedocs.io/en/latest/events/tracker.html)) and the "approximately 7 seconds" of SC2EGSet ([SC2EGSet paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10491788/)) are the same 7.14 real seconds. The tutorial can use `ext_replay_seconds` for every "workers at 6:00" style benchmark without converting.

Three caveats belong in the tutorial text. Zerg morphs (Baneling, Ravager, Lurker, Brood Lord) appear in the tracker as UnitTypeChange events, not births, so a composition built from `unit_born` undercounts them, and UnitDiedEvent fires for a unit "being removed for any reason, including morphing, merging, or being killed", so kill counts from `unit_died` must drop rows without a killer ([sc2reader tracker events](https://sc2reader.readthedocs.io/en/latest/events/tracker.html)). Unit costs, build times and supply are not in the snapshot; per-engagement losses in resources need a static cost table such as the one spawningtool ships ([spawningtool README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StoicLoofah/spawningtool/master/README.md)), although `stats` already gives losses in resources per 7-second window without it. And whether `minerals_collection_rate` is per game minute or per real minute is stated by no source; the tutorial should compare one replay's mean against the client's Income panel before labelling the axis. SQ's inventor adds that Protoss bank before Warp Gate cycles and that late-game banking at max supply lowers SQ unfairly ([Liquipedia, SQ](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/Spending_quotient)), so SQ comparisons across races need that disclaimer.

A coach's checklist, computable from the snapshot and matching what coaching material asks players to look at, gives level 1 its storyline: constant worker production ("every second you do NOT build a worker is a mistake", 66-80 workers in a macro game), bank kept low (Zerg rule "SQ > 80"), never supply blocked, injects, chrono and MULEs on cycle, and reading the graphs before watching the replay ([Liquipedia, How to learn StarCraft](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/How_to_learn_StarCraft); [Liquipedia, Drone Timing](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/Drone_Timing)). TheStaircase supplies concrete benchmarks: 16 mineral workers per base, 48 then 66 workers, at most two queued units per production facility ([Liquipedia, TheStaircase](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/TheStaircase)), and the sc2replaystats Training Center frames improvement as reducing "Idle Production time", "Floated Resources" and "Supply Block time" against the average of the target promotion league ([sc2replaystats, Training Center](https://sc2replaystats.com/blog/?p=147); [Improving by Statistics](https://sc2replaystats.com/blog/?p=1629)). Two of those three are computable here, which is a good framing for why the snapshot is useful and where it stops.

## Win rate by matchup, map and game length anchor the comparisons

The comparisons the community already reads are all `details`-based, with openings joined from `unit_born` and the command tables. Spawning Tool's research pages break win rates down by game-length bucket (0-4, 4-10, 10-15, 15-20, 20+ minutes, for example 35,856 games at 49.82 % in the 4-10 bucket) and by matchup from each side (ZvT 51.16 %, TvZ 48.43 %, ZvP 51.13 %, PvZ 48.46 %, PvT 49.90 %), with filters for race, player, build order, map and date ([Spawning Tool, win rates](https://lotv.spawningtool.com/research/winrates/?tag=4)); its Metagame Report counts community-labelled openings per matchup over the last 30 days, such as Reaper Expand 5, Mech 4, 2 Factory 4 in TvZ ([Spawning Tool, metagame](https://lotv.spawningtool.com/metagame)). Aligulac player pages list win rate against each race in games and in matches with a date filter, and event pages list matchup distributions ([Aligulac player results](https://aligulac.com/players/21244-trigger/results/?page=23); [Aligulac event](https://aligulac.com/results/events/116768-TSL-)). Liquipedia map pages carry per-map matchup statistics and rush distances ([Liquipedia, Ephemeron LE](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/Ephemeron_LE)), and SC2EGSet's dataset-level figures are race and matchup distributions, APM and match-time distributions by race, split by tournament period ([SC2EGSet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10491788/); [arXiv 2207.03428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03428)).

The canonical set for levels 4 and 5 is therefore: win rate by matchup stated from one race's side; win rate per map per matchup; game-length distribution overall, per matchup, per map and per race in the Spawning Tool buckets; opening popularity per matchup per 30-day window; head-to-head and per-opponent-race records per player; and per-player progression over calendar time (SQ, workers at 6:00, win rate by month). Two framing rules matter. Pooled matchup win rates sit near 50 % from each side because every game is counted twice, once per player, so every win-rate figure must say whose perspective it takes. And the "patch" axis is available: the `details` table carries `ext_protocol_version` (UInt32), the build the replay was recorded with, alongside `ext_datetime`; that build number, mapped to Liquipedia's patch pages, is a cleaner period variable than the date, and SC2EGSet's tournament-period split is the precedent for "over time" cuts. Win rates are proportions: the Wald interval collapses near 0 or 1 and overshoots [0, 1], whereas the Wilson score interval is "among the most accurate and robust of the approximations" ([Wikipedia, binomial proportion CI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_proportion_confidence_interval)), and esports analyses filter low-count entities before computing rates "since low-sample champions produce extreme values" ([LoL champion analysis](https://jocelynmiao.github.io/LOL_champ_analysis)); the map and player comparisons should show the Wilson interval and apply a minimum-games filter, with the threshold disclosed.

## DuckDB scans the Polars LazyFrame, so one scan feeds both engines

The project's loader (`/home/seb/git/s2-polars-data-analysis/jupyter_notebooks/snapshot.py`) builds every table as `pl.scan_ipc(root / "year=*/month=*/day=*/<table>.arrow", hive_partitioning=False)`, and the DuckDB twin should consume that same `LazyFrame`. DuckDB's 2.0 documentation states that it "can query a Polars DataFrame or LazyFrame directly by its variable name in the current scope. For LazyFrames, DuckDB pushes down column selections and any filters it can translate, so Polars reads only the rows and columns the query needs" ([DuckDB Polars guide 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/guides/python/polars.html)), and duckdb-python 1.5.0 was the release that introduced "Polars LazyFrame projection and filter pushdown", with 1.5.3 fixing self-joins of LazyFrames and 1.5.4 ignoring Polars dynamic predicates ([duckdb-python releases](https://github.com/duckdb/duckdb-python/releases)). The local run confirms the installed 1.5.2 answers `SELECT count(*), max(food_used) FROM stats` against the project's `stats` LazyFrame (242,393 rows). The pattern per level is:

```python
stats = s2.scan(ipc_dir, "stats")                      # Polars LazyFrame, hive_partitioning=False
pl_res = (stats.group_by("ext_fs_id", "player_id")
               .agg(sq_i=pl.col("minerals_collection_rate").add(pl.col("vespene_collection_rate")).mean(),
                    sq_u=pl.col("minerals_current").add(pl.col("vespene_current")).mean())
               .with_columns(sq=35 * (0.00137 * pl.col("sq_i") - pl.col("sq_u").log()) + 240))
con = duckdb.connect(); con.sql("SET TimeZone = 'UTC'; SET memory_limit = '24GB';")
db_res = con.sql("""
    SELECT ext_fs_id, player_id,
           avg(minerals_collection_rate + vespene_collection_rate) AS sq_i,
           avg(minerals_current + vespene_current)                 AS sq_u,
           35 * (0.00137 * sq_i - ln(sq_u)) + 240                  AS sq
    FROM stats GROUP BY ALL ORDER BY ALL""")
same_rows(pl_res, db_res, keys=["ext_fs_id", "player_id"])
```

Because the loader passes `hive_partitioning=False`, there are no `year`/`month`/`day` columns on either side; the date of a game is `details.ext_datetime` joined on `ext_fs_id`. Replacement scans find variables visible at the `sql()` call, so frames defined in notebook cells (module globals of the kernel) work, frames built inside helper functions must be `con.register(name, lf)`'ed, and `SET python_scan_all_frames = true` restores the pre-1.1.0 behaviour of searching calling frames ([DuckDB data ingestion 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/clients/python/data_ingestion.html)). Only LazyFrames and pyarrow Datasets get pushdown; "Tables, record batches, readers, scanners, and Polars DataFrames are read whole, and the filter applies to what they return", a RecordBatchReader can be read only once, and Arrow inputs "are scanned on all threads, even with a single chunk", so queries without `ORDER BY` can return different rows between runs ([DuckDB data ingestion 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/clients/python/data_ingestion.html)). Results come back with `rel.pl()` (DataFrame), `rel.pl(lazy=True)` (a LazyFrame wrapping a `PYTHON SCAN`, still one DuckDB execution), or `pl.DataFrame(rel)` through the Arrow PyCapsule interface without pyarrow; `pl.from_arrow(rel)` returns a Series of structs, not a DataFrame, and results of `con.execute()` have no `.pl()` ([DuckDB Polars guide 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/guides/python/polars.html)). Prefer an explicit `con = duckdb.connect()` per notebook over the module-level `duckdb.sql`, which shares a global in-memory database and raises `ResourceInUseException` under concurrent use ([DuckDB Python overview 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/clients/python/overview.html)).

**Implementation note (2026-11-04):** this route also stopped working in practice. s2protocol-rs 5.1.0 writes `string_view` columns, which the `arrow` extension rejects on DuckDB 1.5.2 ("Unrecognized Field type with value 24"). `s2.duck()` now registers `pyarrow` datasets with those columns read as `string`, because pyarrow cannot compare `string_view` with `string` when DuckDB pushes a filter down. The UI database copies the snapshot into native tables.

The "DuckDB reads the `.arrow` files itself" route exists but is the wrong default for this tutorial. `INSTALL nanoarrow FROM community; LOAD nanoarrow;` (`arrow` is an alias) provides `read_arrow('ipcs/**/stats.arrow', hive_partitioning = true, filename = true)` over globs and lists, reads both the IPC file and stream formats and lz4/zstd compression ([duckdb-nanoarrow README](https://github.com/paleolimbot/duckdb-nanoarrow); [Arrow IPC support in DuckDB](https://duckdb.org/2025/05/23/arrow-ipc-support-in-duckdb)), but it has no filter pushdown into IPC files (issue #29), no dictionary-encoded columns (issue #25) ([duckdb-nanoarrow issues](https://github.com/paleolimbot/duckdb-nanoarrow/issues)), and its compatibility matrix lists only 1.4.4, 1.4.5, 1.5.5, 1.5.6 and 2.0.0 ([nanoarrow community extension](https://duckdb.org/community_extensions/extensions/nanoarrow)), so an install on the pinned 1.5.2 is unverified. Scanning the LazyFrame reads the same bytes once with the same schema on both sides, which is also what makes the equality checks meaningful.

The equality check is the pedagogical core of the twin cells, and the differences to expect are dtype and order, not value. DuckDB `/` on two integers returns a floating-point result and `//` is integer division, with division by zero raising unless wrapped in `TRY(...)` ([DuckDB numeric functions 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/sql/functions/numeric.html)); DuckDB sorts NULLs last by default while Polars `sort` puts them first ([DuckDB ORDER BY 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/sql/query_syntax/orderby.html); [LazyFrame.sort](https://docs.pola.rs/api/python/stable/reference/lazyframe/api/polars.LazyFrame.sort.html)); `count(*)` is BIGINT against `pl.len()`'s UInt32, integer `sum()` widens to HUGEINT, Arrow uint8/16/32 come in as UTINYINT/USMALLINT/UINTEGER (the snapshot's `player_id` is UInt8 and `ext_fs_id` UInt64), Categorical columns return as VARCHAR, and a timezone-aware Arrow timestamp becomes TIMESTAMPTZ displayed in the session `TimeZone` while the snapshot's naive `Datetime("ns")` stays TIMESTAMP ([DuckDB timestamp types 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/sql/data_types/timestamp.html)). Neither engine guarantees group-by row order: Polars `group_by(maintain_order=False)` by default, and Polars 2.0's streaming engine "does not guarantee row order by default for `join`, `group_by`, and `unpivot`" ([Announcing Polars 2](https://pola.rs/posts/announcing-polars-2/)). The helper that absorbs all of this sorts both sides on the keys with `nulls_last=True`, selects the Polars column order, casts the DuckDB frame to the Polars schema with `strict=False`, and calls `polars.testing.assert_frame_equal(a, b, check_exact=False, abs_tol=1e-9, categorical_as_str=True)`; note `rtol`/`atol` were renamed `rel_tol`/`abs_tol` in Polars 1.32.3 ([assert_frame_equal](https://docs.pola.rs/api/python/stable/reference/api/polars.testing.assert_frame_equal.html)). Order-sensitive DuckDB aggregates (`first`, `last`, `list`, `string_agg`) need an inner `ORDER BY`, as in `list(x ORDER BY y)` ([DuckDB aggregates 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/sql/functions/aggregates.html)).

The SQL dialect question has a clean answer: write "one SQL, two engines" where Polars SQL can run the same text, and "DuckDB-only SQL" where it cannot. Polars has "no separate SQL engine", it translates SQL into expressions and runs lazily ([Polars SQL intro](https://docs.pola.rs/user-guide/sql/intro/)); it supports `GROUP BY ALL`, `ORDER BY ALL`, `QUALIFY`, CTEs, ASOF/SEMI/ANTI joins, eleven window functions with a restricted frame list, `DATE_TRUNC`, `DATE_PART`, `REGEXP_LIKE`, `ARRAY_AGG`, `UNNEST`, `QUANTILE_CONT`, `MEDIAN`, `STRING_AGG` and `FILTER (WHERE ...)` on every aggregate ([Polars SQL clauses](https://docs.pola.rs/api/python/stable/reference/sql/clauses.html); [window](https://docs.pola.rs/api/python/stable/reference/sql/functions/window.html); [aggregate](https://docs.pola.rs/api/python/stable/reference/sql/functions/aggregate.html)), but has no `PIVOT`, `COLUMNS(...)`, `SUMMARIZE`, lambdas, `arg_max`, `histogram` or `mode`. DuckDB's friendly SQL adds `SELECT * EXCLUDE/REPLACE/RENAME`, `COLUMNS('regex')` with `\0` aliases, `PIVOT ... ON ... USING ... GROUP BY`, `SUMMARIZE` (min, max, approx_unique, avg, std, q25/q50/q75, null_percentage) and `arg_max(arg, val[, n])` ([DuckDB friendly SQL 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/sql/dialect/friendly_sql.html); [SUMMARIZE](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/guides/meta/summarize.html); [PIVOT](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/sql/statements/pivot.html)); `SUMMARIZE` is the natural level 0 cell, with `describe()` as the Polars twin. Two dialect pitfalls are version-bound: DuckDB 1.5 changed `date_trunc` on a `DATE` to return a `TIMESTAMP` ([Announcing DuckDB 1.5.0](https://duckdb.org/2026/03/09/announcing-duckdb-150)), and lambda syntax moved to Python-style `lambda x: x + 1`, with the arrow form `x -> x + 1` warning in 1.5, disabled by default in 2.0 and removed in 2.1 ([DuckDB lambda functions 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/sql/functions/lambda.html)). Polars SQL's `DATE_TRUNC` likewise returns a timestamp for a date ([Polars SQL temporal](https://docs.pola.rs/api/python/stable/reference/sql/functions/temporal.html)), so weekly bins should be built once on `ext_datetime` and reused.

Version pins decide how long the notebooks keep running. The project pins `polars[pyarrow]==1.40.0` and `duckdb[all]>=1.5.2`, and the venv has polars 1.40.0, duckdb 1.5.2, Python 3.14.9. DuckDB 2.0.0 LTS ("Cinnamon Teal") shipped on 2026-10-21 with breaking changes, 1.5 support runs to March 2027, and 2.0 removed `.pl()`, `fetch_arrow_table` and `arrow(n)` from DB-API results and introduced a storage format 1.5 cannot read without `storage_compatibility_version = "v1.5.0"` ([Announcing DuckDB 2.0.0](https://duckdb.org/2026/10/21/announcing-duckdb-200); [duckdb-python releases](https://github.com/duckdb/duckdb-python/releases)). Polars 2.0 had its first release candidate on 2026-09-02, makes `collect()` default to the streaming engine, drops the row-order guarantee of `group_by`/`join` unless `maintain_order=True` (joins `maintain_order="left"`), and removes `join_nulls` ([Announcing Polars 2](https://pola.rs/posts/announcing-polars-2/)); the "stable" API docs are already generated from py-polars 2.1.0 ([LazyFrame.collect](https://docs.pola.rs/api/python/stable/reference/lazyframe/api/polars.LazyFrame.collect.html)). The pins to write are `polars>=1.40,<2` and `duckdb>=1.5.2,<2` (or bump to 1.5.6 for the self-join and dynamic-predicate fixes), with every snippet stating that in Polars 1.x larger-than-memory queries need `collect(engine="streaming")` and that in 2.x streaming is the default with `engine="in-memory"` to opt out. DuckDB defaults to 80 % of RAM and all cores and spills GROUP BY/JOIN/ORDER BY to `temp_directory` ([DuckDB configuration 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/configuration/overview.html); [tuning guide](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/guides/performance/how_to_tune_workloads.html)); on the 62 GB corvid-zt a `SET memory_limit = '24GB'` keeps it from fighting the Polars process for the 35 GB full snapshot, and timing comparisons should pin `threads` and `POLARS_MAX_THREADS` to the same number. One Polars 1.40 bug surfaces in the project's own loader: `lf.head(n).collect()` fails on some projections of the many-file scan with "RecordBatch requires all its arrays to have an equal number of rows" when the slice is pushed into the IPC scan, which is why `snapshot.peek()` retries without slice pushdown; the level 0 cells should call `s2.peek()` rather than `head().collect()`. The DuckDB UI (`CALL start_ui()`, DuckDB >= 1.2.1) is a nice extra for live sessions but needs a browser, network access to `ui.duckdb.org` and a writable catalog; keep it behind an environment variable with `CALL start_ui_server()` (no browser) and never call it under `nbconvert` ([DuckDB UI extension 2.0](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/core_extensions/ui.html)).

## functime fits the slope, polars-ds supplies the p-value

functime 1.0.0 (2026-05-03, after 26 months without a release) is a modernisation release: Polars >= 1.0, Python >= 3.11, stable Rust, new `log1p`, `wide_to_long`/`long_to_wide`, probabilistic metrics (`crps`, `interval_coverage`, `winkler_score`) and quarterly frequency, with the 0.9.x API otherwise intact ([functime releases](https://github.com/functime-org/functime/releases); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/functime/)). The repository has had no push since the release and 56 open issues, so the tutorial should pin `functime==1.0.0` (as `pyproject.toml` does) and carry work-arounds itself. The panel format is strict: "the first two columns represent entity and time", values follow, and "the panel DataFrame must be sorted by entity, time" ([functime docs](https://functime-org.github.io/functime/)); preprocessing functions take a DataFrame or LazyFrame and return a LazyFrame. Level 2's output, `["player", "week", "win_rate", "games"]` sorted by player and week, is that panel, and the local verification ran every forecaster the tutorial needs on it with `freq="1w"`: `naive(freq)`, `snaive(freq, sp)`, `linear_model(freq, lags)`, `ridge`, `knn`, scored with `functime.metrics.smape` and ranked by `functime.evaluation.rank_point_forecasts(y_true, y_pred, sort_by="smape")`, with `train_test_split(test_size=0.25, eager=False)` returning LazyFrames that must be collected before `.shape` or plotting.

The feature extractors are the trend tools. The `.ts` namespace registered on `pl.Expr` by `import functime` has 59 methods and works inside `group_by(...).agg(...)` lazily ([functime feature extraction](https://functime-org.github.io/functime/user-guide/feature-extraction/)); the ones a trend level needs, all verified on polars 1.40.0, are `pl.col("win_rate").ts.linear_trend()` (a struct with `slope`, `intercept`, `rss`, where the slope equals the OLS slope on the integer index), `.ts.mean_change()`, `.ts.number_peaks(support=3)`, `.ts.cid_ce(normalize=True)`, `.ts.longest_streak_above_mean()`, `.ts.autocorrelation(1)` and `.ts.cusum(threshold, warmup_period, drift=0.0)`, which returns a per-observation 0/1 flag (on a clean step from 0.5 to 0.7 at index 20 it flagged indices 20 and 31). Two traps: `.ts.count_above(0.5)` returns a **percentage** (82.5 for 33 of 40 points), not a count, and the `.ts.detrend("linear")` expression raises `SchemaError: non-integer dtype passed to int_range: 'f64'` inside `group_by`/`agg` on polars 1.40, while the transformer form `y.pipe(functime.preprocessing.detrend(freq="1w"))` works. `number_peaks`, `cid_ce` and the streak features describe shape (volatility, streakiness) and belong in a "characterise each player's series" table, not in a trend test.

functime gives no p-value and neither library ships Mann-Kendall, so the trend test comes from polars-ds 0.11.2 (`pds.<fn>()`, no namespaces registered in this version), whose `pds.lin_reg_report(*x, target, weights=None, add_bias=False, std_err="se")` returns, per feature, `beta`, `std_err`, `t`, `p>|t|`, the 0.025/0.975 bounds, `r2` and `adj_r2` ([polars-ds docs](https://polars-ds-extension.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)). The verified recipe is `y.with_columns(t=pl.int_range(pl.len()).over("player").cast(pl.Float64))`, then `group_by("player", maintain_order=True).agg(pds.lin_reg_report("t", target="win_rate", add_bias=True).alias("r")).explode("r").unnest("r").filter(pl.col("features") == "t")`; on a synthetic 40-week panel with true slopes +0.004, -0.003 and 0 it returned beta 0.0053 (p = 0.000013, r2 0.41), -0.0031 (p = 0.013) and 0.0009 (p = 0.46), and `pds.kendall_tau("t", "win_rate")` gave 0.45, -0.23 and 0.06 as a direction cross-check. Pass `weights="games"` so a week of two games does not weigh as much as a week of forty (untested locally). In a plain `select` on one series the result is a struct to `unnest` directly, `explode` raises `InvalidOperationError`. `pds.rolling_lin_reg(..., window_size=8)` gives local slopes, and `pds.ttest_ind`, `mann_whitney_u`, `ks_2samp` and `chi2` cover the player and map comparisons of levels 4 and 5 inside the frame.

The honest trends recipe for 10-300 weekly points is: aggregate wins and games per week, plot the rate with Wilson intervals and a games-weighted `rolling_mean_by("week", "8w")`, fit the weighted `lin_reg_report` per entity and report slope, p-value and interval, cross-check the sign with Kendall's tau and `.ts.linear_trend()`, use `.ts.cusum()` only to point at candidate weeks, then confirm by eye. Below about ten weeks say "too short to call" ([EarthSoft, Mann-Kendall](https://help.earthsoft.com/lib_mann-kendall.htm)), and when `.ts.autocorrelation(1)` or `functime.evaluation.ljung_box_test` shows autocorrelated residuals, say the p-values are optimistic ([NOAA IEA](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/62985)). Mann-Kendall proper means a numpy `pymannkendall.original_test` per entity in `map_groups` (returns `trend, h, p, z, Tau, s, var_s, slope, intercept`, [pyMannKendall](https://github.com/mmhs013/pyMannKendall)), and offline changepoints mean `ruptures` (`rpt.Pelt(model="rbf").fit(signal).predict(pen=10)`, [ruptures](https://centre-borelli.github.io/ruptures-docs/)); both leave the Polars-native path and belong in a "beyond" note, not in the level. Patch analyses are a natural capstone: LoL patch studies find champions pulled back toward a 50 % win rate ([WPI, Nerfs, Buffs and Bugs](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~claypool/papers/lol-crawler/paper.pdf)) and describe the post-patch pattern as disruption, volatility, then stabilisation ([winio.ai](https://winio.ai/blog/articles/how-patch-changes-affect-win-probability-in-esports-matches)); with `ext_protocol_version` as the patch key, the same question is askable of SC2 openings.

## Kaleido 1.5 renders PNGs through a downloaded headless Chrome

Static export "requires Kaleido version 1.0.0 or greater", and "starting with Kaleido 1.0.0, Chrome is not bundled" ([plotly, static image export](https://plotly.com/python/static-image-export/)); Kaleido 1.0.0 replaced `kaleido.scopes.plotly` with `write_fig`/`write_fig_sync` and "requires Plotly.py v6.1.1 or later", 1.1.0 added the persistent sync server, and 1.5.0 (2026-10-06) moved to choreographer 1.4.0 and improved the `ChromeNotFoundError` message ([Kaleido releases](https://github.com/plotly/Kaleido/releases)). The venv has plotly 6.7.0, kaleido 1.5.0 and a Chrome 142.0.7444.175 that `plotly_get_chrome` downloaded into `~/.local/share/choreographer/deps/chrome-linux64/chrome` on wormgat, which has no system Chromium. Discovery checks PATH and fixed locations such as `/usr/bin/google-chrome`, and `BROWSER_PATH` overrides it ([plotly, static image export](https://plotly.com/python/static-image-export/)); `pio.get_chrome()`, `kaleido.get_chrome_sync()`, `plotly_get_chrome` and `kaleido_get_chrome` are the four spellings of the same download. Choreographer launches Chromium with `--headless --no-sandbox --disable-gpu`, so root inside a container needs no extra flag. On corvid-zt, where the CI runs, the first `write_image` raises `ChromeNotFoundError` until `plotly_get_chrome` has run once as that user or `BROWSER_PATH` points at a chromium, and inside docker (`--network host` on that machine) the download needs outbound access, or copy `~/.local/share/choreographer/deps` in.

The API is `fig.write_image(path, format=None, width=None, height=None, scale=None)` with the format taken from the extension, `fig.to_image(format="png", width=600, height=350, scale=2) -> bytes` where "width and height are in logical pixels, and scale raises or lowers physical resolution", and the batch `pio.write_images(figs, paths)` for Kaleido v1 and later ([plotly, static image export](https://plotly.com/python/static-image-export/)). Measured on wormgat: one PNG at scale 2 is 62,824 bytes, three PNGs through `write_images` take 0.91 s, and after `kaleido.start_sync_server()` a single `write_image` takes 0.77 s instead of launching and shutting down a Chrome per call. The `engine=` argument still exists but is deprecated, and passing any value warns "Support for the 'engine' argument is deprecated and will be removed after September 2025", twice per call in 6.7.0; `pio.defaults.default_width/height/scale` set notebook-wide defaults, and `pio.defaults.headers` is a 6.8 feature absent from 6.7.0. Plotly Express switches to WebGL `scattergl` above 1,000 points, which exports to SVG/PDF with rasterised parts, so the kill heat maps of level 5 should be PNG. The one hard rule is to install `plotly` and `kaleido>=1.5.0` directly and never `functime[plot]`, whose metadata pins `kaleido==0.2.1`. The notebook preamble that follows from all this is `pio.defaults.default_width, default_height, default_scale = 1000, 500, 2`, `kaleido.start_sync_server()` once, `fig.write_image("figures/<level>_<metric>.png")` at the end of each level cell, and `pio.write_images(figs, paths)` for the comparison grids; the project's `uv.lock` resolves kaleido 1.5.0 and choreographer 1.4.0, and the `pyproject.toml` on disk lists `kaleido>=1.5.0`, so the lock and the manifest agree.

## Conclusion

The research changes the tutorial's premise in one place and strengthens it everywhere else. The premise that supply was missing is false: the snapshot's `stats` table carries `food_used`, `food_made` and the active-forces fields, so the three coaching metrics sc2replaystats built its Training Center on (supply-block time, floated resources, army value) become level 1 and level 4 material, and only APM/EPM and idle-production time stay out of reach for structural reasons. The `ext_protocol_version` column likewise turns "over time" from a calendar cut into a patch cut, which is what Spawning Tool's metagame pages and the LoL patch literature actually study. Writing every query twice is cheap because DuckDB 1.5.2 already scans the project's LazyFrames with pushdown; the pedagogical payoff is not the SQL itself but the equality check, which forces the reader to confront NULL order, integer division, `count(*)` versus `pl.len()` dtypes and the absence of group-by order guarantees, all of which Polars 2.0 is about to make stricter.

The version timing is the risk to manage. Both engines crossed a major-version line within six weeks of the notes (DuckDB 2.0.0 on 2026-10-21, Polars 2.0 in release candidate since 2026-09-02) while functime has been dormant since May and pins nothing above Polars 1.0, so the tutorial should state the pins `polars<2`, `duckdb<2`, `functime==1.0.0`, `kaleido>=1.5.0` in its first cell and annotate the three behaviours that flip in 2.x (streaming default, group-by order, lambda syntax). Written that way, the notebooks are a migration guide as much as a tutorial, and the equality-check helper is the test suite that tells the user when an upgrade has changed an answer.
